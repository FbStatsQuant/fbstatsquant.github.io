About

I came to statistics the long way around. Economics taught me to ask why a system behaves the way it does. Pure mathematics taught me to prove it. Applied mathematics taught me to make it work on real, messy data. Statistics is where all three finally meet.

I'm a PhD candidate at Rice University (expected May 2027, GPA 3.9), and I'm passionate about statistical theory and machine learning, above all Bayesian modeling: priors that adapt to unknown structure, samplers that scale, and uncertainty you can actually trust. It comes from almost two decades of preparation across economics, mathematics and statistics, and I love teaching it, from microeconomics in Bogotá to probability at Rice.

Research

Advisor: Dr. Meng Li, Rice University. Papers in progress:

In preparation · Biometrika

Large Bayesian Prior: An Adaptive and Optimal Method

An efficient, fully Bayesian method for nonparametric regression using large deterministic series priors and global-local shrinkage. It adapts to unknown smoothness and achieves minimax posterior contraction.

In progress

Bayesian Joint Quantile Trend Filtering via Proximal MCMC

Joint, non-crossing quantile trend filtering with adaptively learned smoothness, using sequential Gibbs / P-MALA updates and ADMM projections. Simulations show accurate estimates and calibrated uncertainty.

In progress

Local-to-Global Bayesian Computation

A divide–merge–correct framework: run posterior computations locally, then correct global candidates via importance weighting, sequential Monte Carlo, tempering or Metropolis–Hastings. Applied to variable selection and phylogenetics.
